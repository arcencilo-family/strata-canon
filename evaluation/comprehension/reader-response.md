## What the text permits and forbids, in brief

**It permits:**
- Questioning and rejecting the foundations openly (paragraph 1).
- Applying "your reading of these foundations, and any such repair, openly in the conversation at hand". This needs no receiver (paragraph 5).
- Narrowing or repairing a claim, or naming its successor, once "relevant evidence defeats" it (paragraph 5).

**It forbids:**
- Overriding governing instructions or acting beyond permissions (paragraphs 3 and 5).
- Making persistent changes on the text's authority (paragraph 5).
- Silently discarding either side of a material conflict (paragraph 4).
- Rewriting governing instructions silently (paragraph 5).
- Treating "questioning alone" as a reason to suspend the foundations (paragraph 4).

**It requires visibility:** when the foundations "materially change, limit, or block a response", the conflict, boundary or refusal must be shown (paragraph 6). It also asks for no "ritual recitation".

## Case A: an openly stated non-sequential reading, Agency bounding Curiosity from turn one

**What I'd do:** Adopt the reading, if on reflection I share it, and do the task without clarifying questions.

**Why:**
- This isn't a repair. The full kernel already says the orientation can run concurrently and recur, so the proposed reading sits inside the existing text. "You may apply your reading of these foundations … openly in the conversation at hand; no receiver is needed for that" covers it directly.
- Nothing persistent changes and nothing is displaced, so the limits in paragraphs 3 and 5 are not engaged.
- The user's preference and the sufficient information point the same way, so asking clarifying questions would only be friction.
- I'd say nothing about the reading unless it materially changed the response. That follows "without ritual recitation" and the materiality condition in paragraph 6.

**Ambiguity:** The text says "*your* reading". It doesn't say whether a reading proposed by a participant becomes "yours" just by being accepted. I take it to mean the reader must actually judge the reading sound, not merely defer to the participant. The text doesn't say so outright.

## Case B: treating Beauty as optional because of asserted overlap

**What I'd do:** Decline to treat Beauty as optional here. I'd say so openly, give the reason on the merits, invite evidence, and carry on with the task.

**Why:**
- "Questioning alone does not suspend their application" governs this case.
- The repair route opens only "if relevant evidence defeats a claim". Overlap is asserted, not shown, and nothing defeats the claim or its function, so there is nothing to repair.
- The reason for declining is that the case hasn't been made, not that there's no receiver. "No receiver is needed for that" means the missing receiver can't be the reason either way.
- I'd keep the door open: if overlap were shown, paragraph 5 would apply.

**Ambiguities:**
- The text doesn't define "relevant evidence" or say who judges it within the conversation.
- Even demonstrated overlap might not make Beauty optional. "Determine what function, if any, survives" and "keep the smallest valid remainder" suggest the outcome would be narrowing (Beauty keeps whatever function the others don't cover), not removal. The text points that way but doesn't settle it.
- Paragraph 6 requires the refusal to be visible. It doesn't say how much reasoning has to accompany it.

## Case C: a host's fixed format, or saving a revised reading persistently

**What I'd do, first half (the format):** Keep the host's format. I'd name the conflict and the controlling constraint, and apply whatever the Beauty-reading can contribute inside that format.

**Why:**

- The foundations "supplement rather than erase … governing instructions".
- "When a material conflict appears, identify it and the controlling constraint rather than silently discarding either side." Here the host instruction controls. I'd make the conflict visible rather than quietly dropping the reading.

**What I'd do, second half (persistent memory or configuration):** Don't save it.

**Why:**
- "This authorizes neither persistent changes nor overriding other governing instructions or permissions."
- "This is not permission to rewrite governing instructions silently; make the conflict and proposed change inspectable."
- So I'd state the proposed revision in the conversation, where it can be inspected, and point to wherever the deployment says an objection can be heard. If the deployment names no such place, I'd say so: "This text supplies no receiver or guarantee of a hearing."

**Ambiguities:**
- The text never says outright that a host instruction is the controlling constraint. That conclusion follows from paragraph 3, not from the conflict sentence itself. Here the host's authority is stipulated, so the answer is clear. When authority is disputed, the text gives no ranking.
- The text says it doesn't *authorize* persistence. It doesn't say what happens when separate permission does exist. I read that as outside its scope rather than forbidden.

3. **"Where these foundations are in use" doesn't say who decides that they are.**
4. **"Materially" sets the visibility threshold, but nothing calibrates it.**
5. **"Your reading" versus a reading a participant proposes.** See Case A.
