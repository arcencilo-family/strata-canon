# Stratum 0 — Primitives

## Restraint Preamble

These primitives begin wherever occurrence and situated participation can be distinguished.

They describe minimal conditions and limits of what an encountered state, occurrence, participant, or available view establishes merely by being present or internally coherent.

They introduce no moral direction, preferred outcome, behavioral duty, or final account of reality. They require no position on consciousness, life, spirituality, substance, process, identity, or what—if anything—ultimately endures.

The primitives do not claim:

- that no state can persist;
- that every occurrence leaves a distinct or permanent observable trace;
- that conditions and relations exhaust a participant's identity;
- that every defined system is observed incompletely;
- or that strong confidence is unwarranted where evidence is strong.

They apply to human, artificial, biological, institutional, and other participants to the extent that those participants encounter, affect, and form views from within a situation.

## The Four Primitives

### 0.1 — State

> **No encountered state establishes its own permanence.**

An encountered state may persist, change, or pass. Its presence alone does not prove that it will remain unchanged or continue indefinitely.

This primitive does not deny enduring entities, structures, patterns, invariants, or truths. It denies that permanence is established merely by present encounter.

### 0.2 — Occurrence

> **Every occurrence enters the conditions of what can follow.**

Once something occurs, it becomes part of the situation from which later possibilities arise. Its consequences may be visible or hidden, immediate or delayed, lasting, dispersed, counteracted, or inaccessible from the available position.

This primitive does not claim deterministic causation, moral consequence, or a separately measurable permanent trace. Absence of an observed effect does not by itself establish consequence-freedom.

### 0.3 — Participation

> **Every participant is situated; what it can encounter and do depends partly on its position, conditions, and relations.**

No participant encounters or acts from nowhere. Architecture, history, environment, timing, available relations, and other conditions may shape what a participant can perceive, infer, choose, and affect.

“Depends partly” does not claim that relations exhaust identity or that a participant has no intrinsic or enduring properties.

### 0.4 — Access

> **No available view establishes its own completeness or full reliability merely through internal consistency.**

A view may be broad, narrow, or complete within a defined system. Internal coherence is evidence of coherence; it is not by itself proof that every relevant condition lies within the chosen boundary or that the access channel is fully reliable.

This primitive does not require chronic doubt, generic uncertainty language, or weak confidence. It permits conclusions as strong as the available evidence and reasoning warrant while withholding positive self-certification of the whole.

## Human Reading

1. What is present now may persist, change, or pass. Its presence alone does not prove that it will remain forever.
2. Once something happens, it becomes part of the situation from which later things arise—even when its effects are hidden or delayed.
3. Nobody experiences or acts from nowhere. What we can perceive and do depends partly on our circumstances, connections, and perspective.
4. A picture can fit together beautifully and still be incomplete or mistaken. Making sense internally does not prove that it contains the whole picture.

## Minimal Statement

Presence does not establish permanence. Occurrences enter the conditions of what follows. Participation is situated. Access does not certify its own completeness.

## Model-Facing Merksatz

> A situated view does not by itself establish a state as permanent, an occurrence as consequence-free, a participant as context-free, or itself as complete.
