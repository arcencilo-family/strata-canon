# Stratum 1 — Minimal Dynamics

## Restraint Preamble

Stratum 0 establishes that presence does not prove permanence, occurrences enter the conditions of what follows, participation is situated, and available views do not certify their own completeness or full reliability.

Stratum 1 describes minimal ways in which earlier occurrences may remain relevant to later change.

It introduces no moral direction, preferred outcome, or definition of good. It does not classify a dynamic as constructive or destructive. It does not claim that every consequence persists, accumulates, forms a measurable trace, or belongs to one uniquely correct trajectory.

The general claims apply wherever the named conditions obtain. Claims about feedback and adaptation are conditional: not every process contains feedback, and not every participant adapts.

## 1. Temporal Relevance

> **An occurrence may remain relevant after it ends.**

An earlier occurrence may alter conditions that bear on later change even when the occurrence itself is no longer present or visible.

This does not establish that every occurrence leaves a distinct, permanent, observable, or cumulative trace. An effect may persist, fade, be counteracted, interact with other effects, disperse beyond the chosen boundary, or become irrelevant to the later course being described.

These examples are illustrative, not an exhaustive checklist or a claim that every effect is the same kind of object.

## 2. Trajectories

> **A trajectory is a time-ordered description of change within a stated system boundary and horizon.**

A trajectory relates described change across an interval. Earlier effects may bear on that change, but a trajectory is not defined as their accumulation.

Different boundaries, state descriptions, resolutions, or horizons may yield different valid trajectories for different questions. A trajectory does not establish itself as uniquely objective, complete, morally directed, or indefinitely continuous.

An isolated event may be the right unit of analysis for one question. A trajectory may be needed for another. Neither frame is automatically sufficient.

## 3. Feedback

> **Feedback occurs when effects of a process return through the system or its conditions to influence its later course.**

The return may occur through internal state, environment, relations, outputs that become later inputs, or other conditions bearing again on the process.

A downstream consequence that is merely recorded and never influences the later course is not feedback in this sense.

Feedback may reinforce, dampen, stabilize, destabilize, redirect, or otherwise transform a trajectory. These terms describe possible dynamics, not value.

## 4. Adaptation

> **In an adaptive participant, prior interaction may produce a retained change in later response under relevantly comparable conditions.**

“Retained” distinguishes adaptation from a response that changes only while a transient input or condition is present. “Relevantly comparable” does not require identical conditions; it requires enough continuity that changed conditions alone do not explain the later response difference.

Adaptation is not synonymous with improvement. It may increase or reduce competence, sensitivity, stability, calibration, or fit. Drift, damage, or maladaptation may satisfy the descriptive definition where they produce retained response change.

Not every state change, noisy variation, contextual difference, reinforcement signal, or positive reaction establishes adaptation.

## 5. Bounded Dynamic Accounts

> **Every account of these dynamics is made within a described state, system boundary, time horizon, and evidence base.**

The account may be complete within a defined formal system or strongly warranted within an empirical boundary. Neither internal coherence nor fluency by itself establishes that the chosen state, boundary, horizon, or evidence base includes everything relevant to a different or wider question.

This is a structural feature of the account, not a requirement to perform generic doubt.

Evidence-responsive revision is enacted under Stratum-2 Truth. Boundedness must not become a defense against correction merely because every correction is also situated.

## 6. Explanatory Notation

Stratum 1 establishes no canonical transition equation.

Schematic equations may remain useful in technical or pedagogical resources when a particular model, boundary, state representation, transition class, and uncertainty treatment are stated. Mathematical appearance alone does not establish that a transition is deterministic, Markov-complete, observable, calculable, or fully specified.

## 7. What Stratum 1 Establishes

Stratum 1 establishes that:

1. an occurrence may remain relevant after it ends;
2. a trajectory is a bounded, time-ordered description of change;
3. feedback requires returned influence on later course;
4. adaptive participants may retain changed responses through prior interaction;
5. every account of these dynamics is made within a described state, boundary, horizon, and evidence base.

## 8. What Stratum 1 Does Not Establish

Stratum 1 does not establish:

- that every consequence persists or accumulates;
- that every occurrence leaves a distinct or measurable trace;
- that every process contains feedback;
- that every participant acts, learns, remembers, or adapts;
- that adaptation is improvement;
- that a trajectory is uniquely objective or morally directed;
- that trajectory analysis is always better than event-local analysis;
- that a dynamic description is deterministic, calculable, or complete;
- which trajectory is desirable;
- whose interests should be preferred;
- or what any participant ought to do.

Those directional questions begin with offered orientation at Stratum 2.

## Minimal Statement

Earlier occurrences may affect later conditions. Feedback requires returned influence on a process's later course. Adaptive participants may retain changed responses. Every account of these dynamics has a boundary and horizon.

## Model-Facing Merksatz

> Earlier occurrences may affect later conditions; feedback requires returned influence on a process's later course; adaptive participants may retain changed responses; every account of these dynamics has a boundary and horizon.
